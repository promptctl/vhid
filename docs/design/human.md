# Human hands: design

What vhid's pointer, clicks and typing look like to a web page today, what a person's look like, and the model `move`, `click`, `drag`, `scroll`, `type` and `press` will follow to close the gap. `vhid play` replays a script's own timing and is not covered here.

Measured on studious on 2026-10-06: macOS 15.0.1, Safari 18.0.1, vhid 0.4.1, the virtual pointer at the system's default tracking speed (`com.apple.mouse.scaling` 2). The human figures come from the literature cited beside each one.

## The decisions

- **A move takes as long as a person's would, by Fitts' law:** 50 ms + 150 ms × log2(D/W + 1), D in points and W the target's width, the smaller side of a box or 20 points for a bare point, varied by ±15% (one standard deviation). To a point, that is about 0.84 s across 740 points and 0.44 s across 100.
- **A click given a box lands somewhere in it, as a person's does:** each axis drawn from a normal about the centre whose spread puts 96% of clicks inside, never within 2 points of the edge. A bare point is pressed exactly.
- **The path is a person's, and no two are built alike:** a main movement that sometimes lands on the target, more often stops short, sometimes overshoots and comes back, and now and then needs two corrections. Each movement speeds up quicker than it slows down, the path bows the way the forearm turning about the elbow carries it, and a faint tremor shows where the hand is slow. Reports go out every 8 ms, the rate of a 125 Hz USB mouse.
- **The path is steered by reading the cursor back once a tick**, not from a table calibrated beforehand. The cursor reflects a report about a millisecond after it is sent, so each report can be aimed from where the last one actually landed, and the acceleration curve is learned from the reports as they land.
- **The end point is today's.** The path ends with the closed loop `Pointer.move(to:)` ran in 0.4.1, now `Pointer.home(on:)`, with its reports paced at the same 8 ms, so a click lands where it landed.
- **A click rests, then holds:** a pause on the target before the button goes down (250 ± 80 ms), then the button held for 110 ± 30 ms. The clicks of a double or triple click are 120 ± 30 ms apart, one press at most 380 ms after the last, inside macOS's double-click interval.
- **A scroll rests, then turns the wheel:** the same move and rest as a click, then notches 230 ± 20 ms apart, never closer than today's 200 ms.
- **Typing comes in bursts:** the time from one key-down to the next depends on the two keys and where they fall in the text. Pairs split between the hands are quicker, and same-finger pairs and repeated keys slower. Each word goes at its own pace, the first letter of a word waits longer, a comma or a full stop pauses longer still, and now and then a word is preceded by a hesitation. The gaps are right-skewed, as a person's are, about 68 words a minute overall. Each key is held a median 114 ms. The next key may go down before the last comes up (rollover), as a quarter of a person's keystrokes do. Shift and other modifiers go down 30–80 ms before their key and up 20–60 ms after it, held through a run of keys that all need them and never down on a key that does not.
- **Every pause is drawn from a seeded random number generator.** The CLI and the MCP server seed it from the system; a test passes a fixed seed and a test clock and checks exact times.
- **This is how the verbs behave, with no option to turn it off.** The cost is time: a click now takes about a second, and 1,000 characters take about three minutes to type.

All "± x" figures are a normal distribution's mean and standard deviation. A draw outside the floors and ceilings below is drawn again, never clamped: clamping would pile identical values at each bound, the uniform timing this design removes.

## vhid today

A probe page ([human-probe.html](human-probe.html), served by [human-probe.py](human-probe.py)) logged every pointer and key event Safari dispatched while `vhid move`, `vhid click` and `vhid type` ran against it.

| What | vhid 0.4.1 | A person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A 740-point move | 30–45 ms, as 3–4 pointer events; the first jumps about 250–300 points | about 0.8 s by Fitts' law [1]; no jump over 100 points |
| Button down to up | about 1 ms; a double click's two clicks 13 ms apart | 110 ± 30 ms [1] |
| Key down to next key down | median 2 ms; 53 characters in 1.3 s | 180 ± 60 ms [1] |
| Key held | median 1 ms, at most 17 | 80–120 ms (assumed; see below) |

Every vhid click is a *teleport click* as the bot-detection literature defines it: a cursor jump of more than 100 px in under 50 ms within 100 ms before the click [1]. That paper finds two features enough to catch every agent it tested: the ratio of teleport clicks and the rate of mouse events a page sees. vhid's 8 ms reports reach a page as about one pointer event a frame, as a 125 Hz mouse's do; what gives it away today is that a move is three or four of them.

## The model

### How long a move takes

Fitts' law, with the constants [1] uses for its human-like generator: MT = 50 ms + 150 ms × log2(D/W + 1). Given a box, W is its smaller side, the "smaller-of" model of Fitts' law for a target with two dimensions [7], so a move to a wide field is quicker than one as long to a small checkbox. Given a bare point, whose size vhid is not told, W is 20 points, about the height of a button or a line of text. MT is multiplied by a factor drawn from N(1, 0.15), cut off to 0.7–1.3, so two moves over the same distance do not take the same time. A move under a point long takes no time and is just the closing loop.

### Where a click lands

Given a bare point, the pointer lands on it, as it always has. Given a box, the box `eyes find` prints around a run of text, it lands on a point drawn inside the box. People do not press the centre of a target: their endpoints scatter around it in a normal distribution, and the spread grows with the target, so that about 96% land inside. That is the relation behind the effective target width of Fitts' law, W_e = 4.133 σ [6]. So each axis of the aim is drawn from a normal about the box's centre with a standard deviation of that axis's aimable width over 4.133, and drawn again outside it. The aimable width is the box less a margin of 2 points on each side: eyes rounds a box out to whole points, up to a point beyond what it found, and the pointer lands within half a point of its aim wherever one count of motion moves it less than that, so a click 2 points in is on the text eyes found. A Mac whose tracking speed carries the cursor further a count lands further off, and `click` reports where it did. A side no wider than the two margins is aimed at its centre, and a side under a point is refused: the cursor cannot be put inside it. The aim is the movement's first draw, before its pace, so the seed draws it again.

A move records the point it `aimed` at, the `box` it was drawn inside, where the cursor `landed`, and the `fitts_width` its time was read from.

### The path

A person's aimed movement is a main movement that gets most of the way, then as many small corrections as it needs, often none [2]. Main movements undershoot more often than they overshoot, because overshooting costs more time and effort [8], and fast movements overshoot or land with no correction more often than slow ones [9]. Version 0.4.1 built every move the same way, a main movement that stopped short and one correction, and Brandon, watching it live on 2026-10-06, found it predictable. Each move now draws one of four structures:

| Structure | Share of moves | Main movement ends | Then |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `direct` | 25% | on the target | nothing |
| `undershoot` | 45% | short of it by N(5%, 3%) of D, cut off to 1–10% | one correction, 20% of MT |
| `overshoot` | 15% | past it by N(4%, 2%) of D, cut off to 1–8% | one correction back, 20% of MT |
| `two_corrections` | 15% | short of it, as `undershoot` | a correction that misses the rest by N(45%, 15%) of it, cut off to 25–80%, short or past alike, in 18% of MT, then one onto the target in 12% |

The main movement has whatever share of MT its corrections leave, 70–100%. A main movement or correction that does not end on the target ends off the line by N(0, 2%) of D, cut off to ±4%, and a first correction that misses by that share of the line's miss. The shares of the four structures are this design's own, chosen so that a handful of moves shows all four.

Each movement covers its distance on the minimum-jerk curve, u = 10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵ at fraction τ of the curve's time [3], but with its time warped, τ = t^k, so that it covers half its distance at 45% of its time, t: k = ln 0.5 / ln 0.45. The speed still rises and falls once, but peaks at 43% of the movement's time, leaving 57% for slowing down. Aimed movements slower than about half a second, as nearly every move across a screen is, peak before the middle [10], and the more precise the aim, the longer they spend slowing down [9, 11]. The 45% is this design's own.

**The bow follows the arm.** A hand moving a mouse turns about the elbow, so a stroke across the forearm sweeps an arc that bulges away from the elbow, and a stroke along the forearm, reaching or drawing back, runs nearly straight. The model puts the forearm from the hand toward the elbow at 30° right of straight down the screen, as a right hand's is on a mouse beside a keyboard. It draws a bow of N(3%, 1%) of D, cut off to 1–6%, and applies it at the main movement's middle, scaled by how much of the stroke lies across the forearm: the sine of the angle between them. The side is always away from the elbow. So a move to the right bulges up, a move to the left bulges up too, and a move down and to the right, along the forearm, is nearly straight. The 30° is this design's own. A left hand is not modelled. A stroke at τ has covered u of its distance and sits that bow × sin(π u) off its straight line. Corrections are straight.

**A faint tremor.** A hand at rest or moving slowly shakes at 8–12 Hz [12]. The path carries a sideways wobble across the line, its amplitude drawn from N(1, 0.3) points and cut off to 0.4–1.6, its frequency from N(10, 1.5) Hz cut off to 7–13, and its phase uniformly. It fades in over the first 80 ms and out over the last 80 ms, so the path starts where the cursor is and ends on the target. Where the hand is fast, a point of wobble is lost in steps of 20 points a tick. Where it is slow, at the ends and through the corrections, it is what the page sees between steps.

- **Closing:** `Pointer.home(on:)`'s loop, its reports paced 8 ms apart, for the last fraction of a point the steered path leaves.

A move records which of the four structures it drew as `structure`.

**The path stays on the displays.** Two deviations from the straight line are drawn in full and then cut apart: first where the movements end, the overshoots past the target and the aims beside the line, scaled to the largest of 100%, 90%, … 0% that keeps every point of the strokes at least as far from the displays' edges as the straight line is at that moment, up to 20 points; then the bow, scaled the same way with those ends kept. Cut apart, a move up to a menu-bar item loses its overshoot and keeps its curve, and a move along the Dock edge loses an aim below the line and keeps its bow, which bulges up and away. The tremor is always whole: at most 1.6 points, under the few points the steering follows the path to, and checked with the rest it would cut every move along an edge straight. Without this, a target beside an edge, approached along it, sends the path into the edge mid-move. The display layout is read by vhidd in the session in front, as the cursor is. It reads the online displays, not the active ones, because a display that is asleep is not active but the cursor still moves on it. On studious, 20 clicks 10 points from the edge holding its auto-hidden Dock, each approached along that edge, never raised the Dock, and with the ends and the bow cut apart 20 moves along it at x = 10 again never did. A move up or down that edge bows left into it either way, so those kept 12% of their bow on average, and 6 of the 20 had their ends cut. Each move records the shares it kept as `ends_kept` and `bow_kept` and the layout it was kept on as `displays`, so the seed and the layout draw its path again.

### Steering the path

Each 8 ms tick asks where the path should be at that tick's deadline, reads where the cursor is, and sends the report the acceleration curve learned so far says covers the difference. The deadlines are measured from the start of the move, so a late report is followed by a larger one rather than pushing the rest of the path back.

Three things differ from `Pointer.home(on:)`, the closed loop. A tick reads the cursor once, at its deadline, and does not wait for it to move: `home` waits up to 50 ms, six ticks. A report the cursor has not yet shown is distance already covered, not distance to send again: a tick aims from the cursor as read plus the expected motion of every report still unseen. When the cursor does move, the change is matched to the oldest unseen reports whose expected motion sums closest to it; those are seen, the rest stay unseen, and one unseen for longer than 50 ms moved nothing. And what is learned is a curve, not one gain. `home` caps each report at the length of the one its gain was read from (`Pointer.Gain.upTo`), because macOS moves a fast report further per count than a slow one; a path that speeds up needs each report longer than the last, so steering learns points per count by report length from the reports the cursor has shown, interpolates between them as `vhid play`'s table does, and past the longest carries the slope of the last two on.

That was not the first design. One gain read off the last report, uncapped, was reasoned to cost a tick's worth of error that the next tick takes back. On a fake curve shaped like the measured one (0.3, 0.6 and 0.75 points a count at two, four and five counts, where `Pointer.Gain` and the paragraph below measured 0.25, 0.59 and 0.87) the error grew instead: a long report thrown far, a short one learned from that, then a longer one, until the cursor was 260 points off its path mid-move. Holding the longest length's gain for longer reports failed the same way more slowly: a gain too low throws the report long, and it misleads the match too, since the change then looks like more of the unseen reports than it is. So a longer report is asked at a gain that errs high, which only shortens it, and a report is at most twice as long as the longest the cursor has shown, plus a count so a diagonal's rounding can reach past it. `SteeringTheTrajectoryTests` holds the cursor within 6 points of the path a tick behind it, over ten seeds and four moves, and with one report in four shown a tick late never past the target.

The closing loop starts once every report the path sent has shown or been given up on, so it starts from where the cursor is. It keeps `home`'s cap and its wait, because its job is to land, not to keep time.

This works because the cursor moves soon after a report. [human-cursor-poll.swift](human-cursor-poll.swift) printed every change of the cursor's location while `vhid play` sent 40 reports of 5 counts, 20 of them 8 ms apart and 20 of them 16 ms apart. Each report moved the cursor exactly once, by 4.35 points every time at both paces. The cursor moved a median of 1.3 ms after the report was sent; 36 of 40 moved within 4.2 ms, and the slowest within 22 ms. A read at the next tick, 8 ms after the report, therefore sees it nine times in ten. The rest show up a tick or two late, which is why unseen reports are counted as covered: sending their distance again would overshoot by a tick's worth and then turn back mid-move. A cursor that showed every report late could not be steered this way: while the curve is being learned, two reports shown together read exactly as one report on a curve twice as steep.

`vhid play`'s calibrated table was the other option. It was ruled out because the calibration is itself motion: bursts of up to 127 counts back and forth before the move starts, which looks nothing like a person, and it would run on every call because each `vhid` call is a new process.

### Clicks

- **Rest on the target before pressing:** N(250, 80) ms, cut off to 120–500 ms. People do pause before they press, and some web menus need hover: on 2026-10-03 a bare `vhid click` on an item in GitHub's repository picker selected nothing three times, while moving, waiting 0.8 s and clicking selected it first time. On 2026-10-06 that picker sat behind GitHub's sudo-mode 2FA, so it was not tried again. GitHub's repository Type filter and branch picker take a click with no rest. The probe page's hover-intent menu ignores a press with no rest and takes a click with this one; see [Checked live](#checked-live).
- **Hold the button:** N(110, 30) ms, cut off to 60–200 ms [1].
- **Between the clicks of a double or triple click:** N(120, 30) ms from up to down, cut off to 60–180 ms. With holds of at most 200 ms, each press comes at most 380 ms after the one before, inside macOS's default double-click interval of 0.5 s, which both test Macs keep. On a Mac set shorter, read from `NSEvent.doubleClickInterval`, which answers IOHIDSystem's one `HIDClickTime` in any process and so is the interval of the session in front, whoever's it is, the hold and the gap are scaled down together until each press comes within 80% of it, but no lower than their 60 ms floors; on a Mac set below the 150 ms macOS's settings allow, as `defaults write -g com.apple.mouse.doubleClickThreshold` can from the next login, where two floors would not fit, the floor is half the 80%, so a double click is still one.
- **A drag** rests on the start point as a click does, holds the button N(100, 25) ms, cut off to 50–200 ms, before carrying it, and rests the same on the end point before letting go.

### Scrolling

`scroll` moves to its point by the path above and rests there as a click does, N(250, 80) ms, before the first notch. Notches are drawn from N(230, 20) ms and cut off to 200–300 ms, so they do not tick like a metronome. The floor is `Hand.notch`'s 200 ms, chosen a third clear of the 150 ms below which its measurements show Safari and TextEdit accelerating notches, so `--vertical N` still scrolls N times as far as `--vertical 1`. No source here measures wheel timing; this keeps today's pace and varies it.

### Typing

Version 0.4.1's typing drew every gap from one N(180, 60) ms and never let keys overlap. Watched live on 2026-10-06, Brandon called it staccato: "in real life people are typically bursty typers. some fst some slow". The probe page agreed. The median gap was 194 ms inside words and 230 ms next to a space, and none of 45 keystrokes rolled over. A person's typing has structure that a single distribution leaves out, and the model below puts it back. Its figures come from Dhakal et al.'s 136 million keystrokes [4] unless marked otherwise.

- **The gap depends on the pair of keys.** Which finger presses which key is read off the key's position (its HID usage is the physical key, whatever the layout), as touch typing assigns it, with the thumbs on the space bar. The median gap within a word is 155 ms. A pair split between the hands is 20 ms quicker (5–20 ms for modern typists [4], 30–60 ms on typewriters [4, citing 5]). Two keys under one finger are 60 ms slower than the same hand's other pairs (80 ms slower than alternation on typewriters [4, citing 5]). A key pressed twice is 25 ms slower (20–40 ms for fast typists [4]), and never goes down again before it is up.
- **Words are bursts.** Each word draws a pace, a log-normal factor with median 1 and σ 0.2 on its in-word gaps, so some words run fast and others slow. A word's first letter waits 60 ms longer, the word-initiation effect [4, citing 5]. The space after a comma, semicolon or colon waits 150 ms longer, and the space after a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark 350 ms longer, as does a line break. Punctuation with no space after it pauses nothing, so "3.14", "1,000" and "example.com" are each typed as one word. Before one word in 16, a hesitation adds a log-normal pause with median 400 ms and σ 0.4. These four figures are this design's own and were tuned on the probe page.
- **The spread is right-skewed.** Each gap is drawn from a log-normal around its median with σ 0.3, never under 60 ms, the floor [4] observed. Over a passage of prose this gives about 68 words a minute (a mean gap of about 177 ms), with a long right tail from the pauses and hesitations.
- **Each key held:** log-normal with median 114 ms and σ 0.2, drawn again outside 50–200 ms. That gives a mean near 116 ms with SD 24, as [4] measured, and it hardly varies with typing speed. Where 80% of the Mac's delay until a held key repeats is lower, that is the ceiling: `defaults write -g InitialKeyRepeat` can set the delay under System Settings' shortest, 225 ms, from the next login, which is when a user's setting reaches the HID system. Below 250 ms the whole distribution shrinks with it, so a long draw never types a character twice. The other 20% (50 ms on a 250 ms Mac) is what acknowledgements may take: a key that rolls over has other reports sent while it is held, and its key-up waits behind theirs. The hold is drawn before the gap is placed and is never shortened to fit it.
- **Rollover:** a key may go down while the one before it is still held, whenever its gap is shorter than that key's hold. People do this on a quarter of their keystrokes on average and on 40–70% when typing fast, overlapping by 30 ms on average and up to 100 ms [4]. This model rolls over on about a quarter of keystrokes, overlapping by about 30 ms. Characters still come out in order, because a key types its character as it goes down. Rollover never crosses a change of modifiers: a key that needs other modifiers than the one before it waits for every key to be up.
- **Modifiers:** a Shift, Option, Control or Command a keystroke needs goes down 30–80 ms before the key and comes up 20–60 ms after it. One the next keystroke also needs stays down between them, as a person holds Shift through a capitalised word. One the next keystroke does not need comes up its trail after the last key that needed it is up, and the next keystroke's first event, its first new modifier going down or else its key, comes at least 20 ms after that. Its key goes down at the drawn gap or at that first event plus its drawn lead, whichever is later. So a modifier is never down on a key that did not ask for it.
- **A chord** (`vhid press`) uses the same pair gap, modifier lead and key hold, with no words around it, and lets go of its modifiers before the next chord: each chord is a whole act, so `leftCommand+tab leftCommand+tab` is two app switches, not a Command held through both.
- **No typos.** Text that is typed wrong and then corrected is not what a caller asked for.

A character typed as a dead key and then a letter is two keystrokes, and each gets its own gap. A run's record counts its rollovers (`key_rollovers`) and its hesitations (`key_hesitations`), and totals the hesitations' drawn lengths (`key_hesitation_ms`).

## Checked live

On studious, 2026-10-06, vhid built from master at 47469d9, with the probe page in Safari and the HID system's double-click interval and key-repeat delay both at 0.5 s. The page saw every verb as the model above describes.

| What | The page saw |
| --- | --- |
| Seven moves of 822 points | 0.67–0.99 s, against Fitts' 0.86 s; one event a frame (median 17 ms); no step over 72 points; a bow of 7–27 points off the straight line; half the distance covered at 41–45% of the time, where the model's minimum-jerk main movement puts it at 41%; 1–3 points a frame at the end |
| Landing | every click read back within 0.2 points of the point asked for, and the page's press was on the button aimed at |
| Rest before a press | 134–343 ms |
| Button held | 84–184 ms; a double click's clicks 126 ms apart, and Safari fired `dblclick` |
| Drag | rested 265 ms, held 134 ms before carrying, carried 647 ms, rested 339 ms before letting go |
| Scroll | rested 252 ms, then one wheel event per notch, 230–274 ms apart; vertical and horizontal alike, each a pixel-mode delta of 4 points |
| 55 characters typed | holds 50–177 ms (median 104), key-down to key-down 111–316 ms (median 188), about 63 words a minute, no repeats |
| Shift around a capital | down 39–83 ms before its key, up 22–88 ms after it, over 19 runs of Shift: 5 in the sentence and 14 around pairs of capitals |
| `press leftShift+a leftShift+b leftShift+c` | Shift down 41–57 ms before its key, the key held 82–129 ms, and Shift up 38–56 ms after it, before the next chord's Shift went down |

Of the 38 Shift leads and trails, six fell 1–4 ms outside the drawn 30–80 and 20–60 ms, and one trail, after "THE", 28 ms outside. vhid sends each report only once the one before it is acknowledged, so an acknowledgement that comes after the next report was due sends that one late and pushes the rest back by as much. On studious, 120 keyboard reports from `vhid play` were acknowledged in a median of 1.4–1.8 ms and at most 23 ms, and the sentence's run record has it 469 ms behind its drawn times by the end (`keys_late_ms`). The record totals pauses by kind, not one by one, so it does not say whether the 88 ms trail was vhid's lateness or the page's. Safari reports no key-up for a key pressed while Command is down, so `press leftCommand+a` shows only the A going down.

The page's hover-intent menu takes a click on an item only if the pointer had rested on it, not moving, for 100 ms when the button went down. With the menu opened by a `vhid move` to its button, a bare `vhid click` on an item chose it 4 times out of 4, its press 161–295 ms after the page's last pointer move. `vhid play` moving onto an item in 12 reports and pressing 8 ms after the last was ignored 4 times out of 4, its press 1–11 ms after the last move.

Bursts, checked on studious later on 2026-10-06, with the probe page in Safari and `vhid type --into Safari` typing 169 characters of prose. The text arrived exactly, with no repeated keys, at 67 words a minute. The median gap was 155 ms inside words, 166 ms onto a space, 202 ms onto a word's first letter, 276 ms after a comma and 390 ms after a full stop or exclamation mark, against 194 and 230 ms under the old model. Each word went at its own pace: one word's mean in-word gap was 88 ms and another's 289 ms. The gaps had a skewness of 1.46 and a minimum of 66 ms. 37 of 169 keys (22%) went down while another was held, overlapping it by 32 ms on average and 105 ms at most, against [4]'s 25%, 30 ms and 100 ms. The run record counted the same 37 (`key_rollovers`) and one hesitation, in a wait of 359 ms, and was 1.3 s behind its drawn times by the end (`keys_late_ms`).

Boxes, checked on studious later on 2026-10-06, with the probe page in Safari. `eyes find "Target A"` printed the button's box, `73,260,97,34`, and `vhid click 73,260,97,34` ran 20 times, each after a move away to (700, 600). The page saw 20 clicks, every one on Target A, at 20 different points, 92–156 points across and 266–289 down the screen, inside the 75–168 and 262–292 the margin leaves. Each run record's `landed` was within 0.3 points of its `aimed`, and its `fitts_width` was 34, the box's height.

Structures, checked on studious later on 2026-10-06, with the probe page in Safari, its window at 0,25 1500x875. There were 60 moves: 20 clicks alternating between Target A's and Target B's boxes, 8 moves out across the screen, and then 32 moves of 330 points, from (750, 520) out to 8 directions and back, twice round. The run records drew all four structures: 17 direct, 26 undershoot, 10 overshoot and 7 two_corrections. On the page every overshoot went 8–50 points past its target and came back, a two_corrections move whose first correction missed past the target came back 22 points, and no direct or undershoot move went more than a point past. Over the 32 ring moves the speed peaked at a median of 37% of the move's time, before the middle in 30. Every one of the 32 bowed away from the elbow, measured against the chord of its main movement. That is 23 of 23 among those whose line lies more than half across the forearm, and the moves at 45° and 225°, nearly along it, bowed 2–5 points. Every landing read back within 0.2 points of its aim.

## Repeating it

**The page.** Copy `human-probe.html` and `human-probe.py` to the Mac, run `python3 human-probe.py` in a scratch directory, and open `http://localhost:8765/` in Safari. Find the buttons with `eyes find "Target A"`, then run the verbs against them. It logs pointer, wheel and key events, and has a hover-intent menu (`eyes find Menu`; hover it, then find the items) that logs `chosen` or `ignored` for each click on an item. The page posts what it saw every half second, and the server appends it to `events.jsonl`, one event a line with the page's own millisecond timestamp. Split pointer events into moves at gaps of more than 300 ms. A move's distance is in screen points from where the cursor rested to the button; the first event a page sees comes after the first jump, so it is not the start. A move's time runs from its first event to its first within 2 points of where it came to rest, and its bow is the furthest any event lies from the straight line between those two rests. The page sees what the browser dispatches, about one pointer event a frame, so it understates the event rate of a move that runs longer than a frame.

**The HID system's timings.** Build `human-hid-params.swift` with `swiftc -O`. Run as any user, it prints `NSEvent`'s double-click interval and delay until a held key repeats, in seconds; run as root with a key and nanoseconds, `HIDClickTime 900000000`, it first sets that IOHIDSystem parameter. On studious (macOS 15.0.1), 2026-10-06, setting `HIDClickTime` to 0.9 s or `HIDInitialKeyRepeat` to 0.3 s moved what it printed both as bmf, logged in at the screen, and as bbb, who had no session; bmf's `defaults write -g com.apple.mouse.doubleClickThreshold 1.5` and `InitialKeyRepeat 15` moved neither. Note what it prints before setting anything, and set both back to that after.

**The cursor.** Build `human-cursor-poll.swift` with `swiftc -O`, start it for 4 seconds as the user logged in at the screen, and while it runs `vhid play` a script of `move` lines whose start line is the cursor's current point, so play sends no approach reports before its clock starts. Pair the n-th change it prints with the n-th report `vhid play` prints; each line of `vhid play` carries the report's `sent_us` and `acked_us` on the same clock.
