# Changelog

Each release's section is its GitHub Release notes, which keep line breaks, so each paragraph and item is one line. `[Unreleased]` lists what `master` has that the latest release does not; at a release its items move under the new version's heading and the empty heading stays, and the link definitions at the foot gain the new version's and compare `[Unreleased]` from its tag.

## [Unreleased]

- `vhid mcp` is the one MCP server: it serves `look`, which reads the screen, beside `type`, `press`, `gesture`, `click`, `move`, `scroll`, `drag`, `play`, `cursor` and `doctor`. `eyes mcp` is gone, and with it the tools `windows`, `displays`, `find`, `read`, `hit` and `grants`, which `look` and `doctor` answer. The Claude Code plugin runs `vhid mcp` alone. A client that was set up with `eyes mcp` by hand drops it: `claude mcp remove eyes`.
- `look` with no `text` answers what is on screen in one call: the displays, the windows front to back, and the text in the place with the point `click` takes for each run. When the place cannot be read, the displays and windows are still there, and the text's place says why: `No text was read: …`. With `text`, it finds the text as `find` did, `near`, `until` and `timeout` included; with `x` and `y`, it says what a click there would land on, as `hit` did. `vhid look` is the same on the command line.
- `look` asks nothing of the daemon, so it waits behind no `click` or `type`: only the acts take turns with one another, and only theirs record `queued_ms`. It reads with the driver extension off.
- `look` takes `app`, an app's name as the windows list prints it, and reads that app's windows and what is stacked in front of them, and so do `eyes find` and `eyes read`, as `--app`. `app` is a place like `display`, `window`, `page` and `rect`, and a look is given at most one.
- Named no place, a look whose frontmost app has no window open, such as Finder over the desktop, reads the app of the frontmost window behind it, and its scope line names both, where it read the windowless app and found nothing. Each place that cannot be read is said on its own: an app not running, an app with no window, a window on no display, and no display active - none attached, or each asleep - where a sleeping display was said as none attached.
- `vhid doctor`, and its MCP tool, end with whether Screen Recording and Accessibility are granted and the app macOS charges them to, which is the app to switch on. The verdict and exit status are still the devices' alone, so a missing grant does not make vhid `not ready`. A grant reading that fails is said as unknown, with why. The plugin's session-start check says a missing grant, with those rows.
- Every unit of work of `vhid` and `eyes` is recorded the same way, with `span_id` and, inside another unit, its `parent_span_id`, on its trace: a `look` call's record has its `overview`, `front` and `reading` units on one trace under it. `eyes`' records say `failed` where they said `error`, keep what a unit found in `attributes.result`, where `outcome` said it, and their `facts` are `attributes`. `vhid`'s `entry` is `attributes.entry`. `eyes`' unit `look` is called `reading`, and its `front` unit says how the app was `chosen`: `in_front` or `named`. `eyes`' events.jsonl is created readable by its owner alone, as `vhid`'s is, and one made by an earlier eyes is made so at its next record. An id is an attribute, where `eyes` counted it: `front_pid`, `display`, `pid` and `window`. A look asked no `role`, and an app with no name, leave the field out, where `eyes` recorded `none` or an empty name, and `passed_over` names a windowless app as the scope line does, with its pid.
- A look's record says each exclusion and each box it could not check as one attribute, `excluded` and `boxes_unchecked`, each with every reason and its count, zeros included, where it counted `excluded_<reason>` and `boxes_unchecked_<reason>` for the reasons seen. `queued_ms` is a count, `counts.queued_ms`, beside a look's `vision_queued_ms`, where it was an attribute. A `doctor` that finds the Mac not ready, and a `read-grants` that reads a grant not held, are recorded `ok`, as a verdict and not a failure, and exit with their status as before. A device hand-back that fails is noted on the verb's record as `leave_failed`.
- `vhid` prints a point as `look` prints one, `x,y` with every digit it has: `clicked left once at 812.5,604`, where it said `at (812.5, 604)` and cut each coordinate to six digits. A box is refused, and said, as `look` spells one: `x,y,width,height` in points, each side at least a point and no edge past 1000000, its far edges included, and a box `vhid` names is printed in the whole points that cover it. A coordinate past 9007199254740992, where a Double stops holding every whole point, is no place for either: `vhid`, `eyes hit` and `play` refuse it in the same words, and a frame an app claims past it, or with a side under zero, leaves the element unread. `look --rect` refuses an empty box. A row for a frame under a point across prints the whole point inside its box, where it grew the box to hold its rounded centre.
- `look` gives up on a screen capture that does not end within 30 seconds, as every command vhid runs is, and a look withdrawn mid-capture ends the capture before it answers.

## [0.6.0]

- `eyes find` and `eyes read`, and their MCP tools, say a read that stopped short in the scope line's first words, `1 matched "Add" in the part read of rect …` and `"Add" not found in the part read of …`, where only its last clause said it, `stopped after 5s` or `parts left unread`: a match from part of the region may not be the one meant, and an absence from it proves nothing. A cut answer, `stopped at the limit of N`, read the whole region and is not said so. Each look's record notes its `reach`: `whole`, `result_limit`, `element_limit`, `time_budget` or `unread`, and for a merge that did not read whole each reader's, `tree_blind+pixels_whole`.
- `eyes find` matches an element by everything it says in words, its value, title and description, where it matched only the first: a button drawn "+" and described "Add", the one an agent could not find in System Settings, is found by `find Add`. Each row from the accessibility tree prints those other words after its role, each after the attribute that says it, `description Add`, so `read` shows what each control is called; the row's text is still the first, the one drawn. A checkbox's 0 or 1 and a link's address are not words to find it by. `eyes hit` reads an element's texts through the same conversion as `find`, and prints each after its attribute, `value 1`, `description Add`, the state and address among them. Each look's record counts the matches made by those other words as `matched_also`. Every text on a row is printed on one line and cut past 120 characters, as `eyes hit` prints them, and a near miss's edits off follow its role.
- `eyes find` and `eyes read`, and their MCP tools, take `--role` (`role`), which keeps only rows of that role, such as `AXButton`. A match of another role is counted `other_role`, and one only the pixels reader saw, whose role nobody knows, `role_unknown`, which leaves the region not read whole, so a role find under the default merged source does not prove an absence where pixels saw the text; with `--source tree` it can. `--source pixels` refuses a role. Each look's record notes the `role` asked, or `none`.
- A cell and the same-text link at the same place are no longer one row before a role is asked: the duplicate is dropped after the match and the role, so `--role AXLink` finds the link. Of the accessibility tree's same-text, same-place pairs, `duplicate` now counts only those the reading would otherwise have answered, where it counted every one in the region, and `other_role` and `role_unknown` count one place once.
- `eyes hit X Y` and the MCP tool `hit` (`x`, `y`, as vhid's `click` takes them) say what a click at a point would land on, by the system's own hit test, before anything is pressed: the app, whether a look with no place named reads it (the front app, a window over it such as an Open panel, the app eyes answers to, an app behind, a display that look does not read, or none of the app's windows, such as its menu bar), its window by the id `--window` takes, then the element hit and each holding it up to the area that holds them, with each one's role, frame and texts. It needs Accessibility and refuses nothing. Where no element answers, it names the window listed frontmost at the point. Each question is a `hit` record with the app, its `standing`, the `front` app a look reads, how the climb `end`ed and the `pid`, `window`, `rows`, and the `calls` and `front_calls` the climb and the front look's rule made.
- `eyes find` and `eyes read`, and their MCP tools, named no place, read what is in front: the frontmost app's windows and whatever is over its front window, such as an Open panel, where they read the main display, every app on it. When the frontmost app is the terminal or app eyes runs in, which shows the very text an agent asks eyes to find, they read the app behind it and the scope line says it passed over; that app's windows are never read, in front or not. A window over the front one is read only where a click there lands in it, so Notification Center's full-screen window is not. The pixels reader counts text in windows behind as `outside`, and a pixels read whose hit tests do not answer, as without Accessibility, says parts were left unread. `--display` reads every app on a display, as before. Each choice is a `front` record naming the app, its pid, the display, the app passed over and how many processes were matched as the one eyes runs in (`own_pids`), and each look's record counts the processes it read as `owners` and every exclusion as `excluded_<reason>`.

## [0.5.0]

- The Claude Code plugin is installed and updated from the newest release, not from `master`: its skills and session-start check are that release's, written for the binaries it ships. Until this release, a plugin installed after a change on `master` could describe tool arguments no released vhid took, such as `click`'s `box` under 0.4.1.
- The Claude Code plugin's session-start check says when the installed `vhid` or `eyes` is not the version the plugin was written for, naming each and the version it reported, with the step for each case: `brew upgrade --cask vhid` for older binaries, `claude plugin marketplace update vhid` and `claude plugin update vhid@vhid` for newer ones, and `brew reinstall --cask vhid` for a pre-release or development build, which no plugin release is written for. The version lives in `claude-plugin/VERSION`, where `VERSION` was, so the installed plugin carries it.
- `vhid mcp` and `eyes mcp` end the instructions they answer a client's initialize with by naming themselves and their version, `This is vhid 0.5.0.`, the version `--version` prints, so an agent reads which release it is driving; the instructions say boxes arrived in 0.5.0.
- `vhid click`, `move`, `scroll` and `drag`, and their MCP tools, take a box as well as a point: `x,y,width,height`, the box `eyes find` prints beside each point (the MCP tools' `box`, and a string for `drag`'s `from` and `to`). Given a box, the pointer lands on a point drawn inside it, spread about the centre as a person's clicks are, held to the line it comes in on as a hand keeps to its direction, so a move down onto a wide menu row comes straight down, and never within 2 points of its edge (a side too narrow for that is aimed at its centre, and one under a point is refused), and the move takes as long as Fitts' law gives for a target that size. A point is pressed exactly, as before. Each move's `paths` entry has where it set out `from`, the point it `aimed` at, the `box` it was drawn inside, where it `landed`, and its `fitts_width`.
- `eyes find` and `eyes read`, and their MCP tools, print each row's box beside its point: `x,y,width,height` in the same screen points, the form `--rect` takes, covering the run and holding the point. The text and role move one column right. `--rect` takes a box that starts with a minus, as one on a display left of the main one does, where 0.4.1 took it for a flag. A row the accessibility tree found is cut to the part of its element's frame where the system's hit test lands a click on that element: Safari gives a page's native-looking button a frame several points wider than the button, and a click inside that frame pressed the page beside it. A rounded button's corners are checked too. The scope line says how many boxes were cut and how many could not be checked and why, and each look's record counts them as `boxes_narrowed` and `boxes_unchecked_unanswered`, `_elsewhere` and `_over_time`, with the `box_calls` the checks made and the `box_ms` they took.
- `eyes windows` and `eyes displays`, and their MCP tools, print bounds as `x,y,width,height`, the form `--rect` takes, where they printed `x,y WxH`, and so does the scope line of `eyes find` and `eyes read` for the region it read. A script that reads the old form must split on commas alone.
- `vhid move`, `click`, `drag` and `scroll`, and their MCP tools, move the pointer as a hand does: a report every 8 ms along a path that speeds up and slows down, sometimes lands, more often stops a little short and corrects, now and then overshoots and comes back, with a slight tremor across the line, kept clear of the displays' edges so a move along one does not bring up an auto-hidden Dock, its curve and the shape of its speed drawn afresh each move so some run straight, taking about as long as Fitts' law says a person would, about 0.8 s across 740 points. In 0.4.1 a move of any length was three or four jumps in under 50 ms. Where the button goes down is read back and lands as near the point as it did. Each run's record has the `seed` its moves were drawn from and each move's `paths` entry, with how it was built as `structure`, the curve and speed profile it drew as `bow` and `halfway`, the `displays` it was kept on, and how much of its curve and its ends it kept there as `bow_kept` and `ends_kept`. `vhid play` replays its script's own motion, as before.
- `vhid scroll --vertical N` and `--horizontal N`, and the MCP tool `scroll`'s `vertical` and `horizontal`, scroll N times as far as 1: each notch is its own report, 200 to 300 ms after the last, varied, so a roll of N takes about N × 230 ms. In 0.4.1 the ticks went out up to 127 to a report, and macOS reads each report as a single notch, so in Safari a roll of any size barely moved the page.
- `vhid type` and `press`, and their MCP tools, type as a typist does, about 68 words a minute: each key held about 114 ms, the keys of a word about 155 ms apart and each word at its own pace, longer pauses between words and after punctuation, and the next key sometimes down before the last is up, though never across a change of modifiers or between chords. 1,000 characters of prose take about 3 minutes. In 0.4.1 the keys went out back to back, so a script or MCP client that times out a long `type` needs a longer timeout. Each run's record counts its `key_rollovers` and totals its keyboard pauses as `modifier_down`, `key_down`, `key_up` and `modifier_up`.
- `vhid click` and `drag`, and their MCP tools, rest on the point about a quarter of a second before the button goes down and hold it about a tenth of one, and the clicks of a double or triple click come about 120 ms apart, inside the Mac's double-click interval. `scroll` rests before its first notch. In 0.4.1 the button went down the moment the move ended and came up a millisecond later, and web menus that take a click only on an item the pointer has rested on ignored it. Each run's record totals its `pauses` by kind.
- When `vhidd`'s file is replaced while it runs, every cursor or display read is refused with an error naming the replacement, until vhidd is restarted, where 0.4.1 started a cursor reader of the other build.
- Every `vhid` run and every MCP tool call leaves one JSON record, with its outcome, its duration and the reports and notches it sent, in `~/Library/Logs/vhid/events.jsonl`, or sent to the OpenTelemetry collector `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` names. Only a run ended by a second signal leaves none. `docs/cli.md` in the repository describes the record's fields.
- Control-C or `SIGTERM` stops a `vhid` verb, which is recorded `cancelled` with what it had sent and `attributes.signal` naming the signal, says so on stderr, and dies by the signal. `doctor`, `driver` and `service` are stopped the same way, ending the command they were running to read the Mac, and so is a `doctor` MCP call that is withdrawn. `vhid mcp` withdraws the tool calls it is running or has queued, as a client's cancel would, and each call's record names the signal. `vhid record` still answers the signal by finishing and printing the script it recorded, and is cancelled, printing none, only when signalled before the tap app is recording.

## [0.4.1]

- Homebrew 7 no longer warns that the cask calls the deprecated `postflight` on every brew command that reads it: the cask copies what uninstall needs at install, with `postflight_steps`.

## [0.4.0]

- `vhid type` and `vhid press` take `--into <app>`, and the MCP tools `into`: with another app in front just before the first key, nothing is sent and the refusal names the app that was. The app is named as `eyes windows` prints the frontmost application. They refuse the same way at the login window or with another user's session in front.
- `vhid gesture <name>` does what a trackpad gesture does, by pressing the key that does it: back and forward, zoom in and out, Look Up, the next or previous Space, Mission Control, App Exposé, Show Desktop, Launchpad and Notification Center. A system shortcut is the one the user has set, and one that is off is refused, naming the setting. `vhid mcp` serves it as the `gesture` tool.
- `vhid mcp` and `eyes mcp` answer a client's initialize with instructions: each names the other server, says the point `find` prints is the point `click` takes, and teaches the look, act, look again loop, so a client without the Claude Code plugin learns the pairing too.
- The Claude Code plugin has a second skill, `menu-item`, which invokes any app's menu item by its name: it searches for the item from the app's Help menu and checks the result before choosing it, and clicks down the menu path for an item in the Help menu itself.
- `eyes find` and `eyes read` print each row's role, such as `AXButton`, or `pixels` for text only the pixels reader saw, as a third column before a near miss's edit count.
- `eyes find --page <window id>` and `eyes read --page <window id>` read only the web page a browser window shows, not its toolbar and bookmarks. The MCP tools take `page`.
- `eyes find --near <text>` orders matches by how close each sits to that text, so `eyes find Remove --near Beta --limit 1` gives the Remove button in Beta's row. The MCP tool takes `near`.
- Over ssh, `eyes grants` names `sshd-keygen-wrapper`, the program macOS holds the grants under, where it named `sshd-session`, whose switch does nothing.
- In Chrome, `eyes find` no longer answers an element scrolled out of view as a match on the viewport's edge, where a click misses it.
- Removing the driver, by `vhid-uninstall --driver` or `brew uninstall --zap --cask vhid`, no longer fails in the moment after a deactivation while macOS tears the driver down; `vhid driver` and `vhid doctor` name that moment `withdrawing`.
- The Homebrew cask reaches this release, and with it the `brew uninstall --zap` and `brew uninstall` behavior 0.3.0's notes describe: 0.3.0 itself never reached the cask, which stayed at 0.2.0.

## [0.3.0]

- A Claude Code plugin, `/plugin marketplace add promptctl/vhid` then `/plugin install vhid@vhid`, adds the `vhid` and `eyes` MCP servers, a skill for the look, act, look again loop, and a check at the start of each session that tells the agent when vhid is missing or not `ready`.
- `brew uninstall --zap --cask vhid` removes the pqrs driver package with vhid, refusing as `vhid-uninstall --driver` does, for instance while Karabiner-Elements is installed. On an install made or upgraded with this release's cask, `brew uninstall --cask vhid` also works after `vhid-uninstall` has been run by hand.

## [0.2.0]

- vhid is a Homebrew cask: `brew install --cask promptctl/tap/vhid` installs the newest release's pkg, and `brew uninstall --cask vhid` removes it as `vhid-uninstall` does.
- `vhid click`, `move`, `scroll`, `drag` and `cursor` work at the login window and behind fast user switching: vhidd reads the cursor in the session in front. In 0.1.0 a cursor read made there answers (0, 0), so those verbs cannot place the pointer.
- `vhid type` and `vhid press` take `--layout <input source id>`, and the MCP tools `layout`, to type on a keyboard layout other than the caller's. With none named and the system reporting no current layout at all, they type with US English; in 0.1.0 they refuse.
- `eyes find --until present|absent` waits for text to appear or go, for as long as `--timeout` allows, on the command line and as the MCP tool.
- `eyes grants` says whether Screen Recording and Accessibility are held and which app macOS charges them to, without prompting; `eyes grants --ask` raises macOS's dialogs. `eyes mcp` serves `grants` as a tool.
- A grant switched on while `eyes mcp` is running counts from its next call. In 0.1.0, quit and reopen the client after granting.
- The menu bar item's **Set Up vhid…** walks the requirements that are not met, one page at a time.
- `eyes` emits one JSON event per look and per grant reading, to the OTLP collector `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` names or to `~/Library/Logs/eyes/events.jsonl`.
- The accessibility tree reader no longer takes Notification Center's full-screen widget window as covering every window behind it.
- Both MCP servers answer every request once, and a session whose input has ended waits for the calls still running.
- Installing over a running daemon waits for launchd to let go of its job before loading the new one, which launchd had refused with error 5.
- vhidd names itself `vhidd` in everything it prints and logs.
- A command vhid or vhidd runs, such as `pkgutil`, `systemextensionsctl` or `ioreg`, is stopped at a time limit and named in the error. In 0.1.0 one that never ended left a client with no answer and vhidd's bring-up loop waiting for good.
- When the driver extension stops while vhidd is serving, vhidd takes the devices down, refuses every verb with the reason and the driver's step, and brings them up again once the driver is back, with no restart. In 0.1.0 `vhid doctor` went on printing `ready` and, while the driver stayed off, the verbs typed and moved nothing.
- vhidd brings the devices up within seconds of the driver extension being turned on: it looks at the driver every two seconds while it waits to try again.
- A verb refused while the driver extension is off names the step the driver is at now. In 0.1.0 it names the step read when vhidd last tried, for up to a minute after that step is done.

## [0.1.0]

The first release: a virtual keyboard and mouse that macOS sees as hardware, driven from the command line or over MCP, and a screen reader that finds what to click. Requires macOS 15 or later.

- `vhid type`, `press`, `click`, `move`, `scroll` and `drag` drive the two virtual devices, and `vhid cursor` says where the pointer is.
- `vhid play` replays a timed script of keyboard and mouse acts; `vhid record` writes that script from the physical keyboard and mouse.
- `vhid doctor` names every requirement the verbs need and the step left for any that is not met; the menu bar item shows the same reading.
- `eyes windows`, `displays`, `find` and `read` say what is on screen and where, in the coordinates `vhid click` takes.
- `vhid mcp` serves `type`, `press`, `click`, `move`, `scroll`, `drag`, `play`, `cursor` and `doctor` as MCP tools, and `eyes mcp` serves `windows`, `displays`, `find` and `read`.
- `vhid --version` and `eyes --version` print the release, and both MCP servers report it.
- One signed, notarized pkg installs both CLIs, the root daemon that owns the devices, the menu bar item, the `vhid record` tap, an uninstaller, and the pinned Karabiner-DriverKit-VirtualHIDDevice package.
- After installing, turn on `org.pqrs.Karabiner-DriverKit-VirtualHIDDevice` under System Settings > General > Login Items & Extensions > Driver Extensions; no installer can do this for you. `vhid doctor` prints `ready` once everything vhid needs is met.
